01 · Snapshot

I helped redesign MyPlate so logging a meal and setting a goal actually felt doable.

Challenge

MyPlate had solid advice from the USDA, but you couldn't log a meal, the goals were the same for everyone, and there was no real way to see how you were doing. In our first test, 2 of 5 people got stuck on basic tasks.

Solution

I focused the redesign on what people asked for most: easier food logging, goals that fit the person, and progress you can check by day, week, or month.

My role

I was one of five on the team. I led the feature design, ran a survey with 38 people to rank features, built the prototype, and ran the concept test.

Redesigned meal logging

02 · Problem

Healthy eating shouldn't feel this complicated.

40%

of participants failed to complete essential tasks in round-one usability testing, limiting the app’s ability to support consistent habits.

01Key actions were hidden, so users had to hunt for them.
02No simple way to log what they actually ate.
03Generic targets that didn't feel like their own.
04No sense of whether they were making progress.

For a habit-building app, it wasn't helping anyone build habits.

Key findings
01

Trust wasn't the problem

People trusted the USDA advice. 68% of reviews were still negative, and they were about the app, not the content. So we kept the guidance and rebuilt the experience around it.

02

Nobody asked for streaks

Badges, streaks, and social sharing ranked last in our survey. People wanted guidance and control, so we left gamification out.

03

“Personal” meant how you eat

“It doesn't feel like it accounts for my being vegetarian,” one tester told us. Goals had to fit someone's diet, not just their numbers.

04 · Design

What I changed, and why.

Decision · 01

Flexible food logging

Insight

People wanted an easy way to log what they actually ate.

Decision

Give people four ways to add food: pick the meal, snap a photo, search, or type in something custom.

Outcome

It works just as well for a packaged snack as for a home-cooked dinner.

4 ways to log

Start by picking the meal: breakfast, lunch, dinner, or snacks.

Decision · 02

Personalized goals

Insight

A generic list of goals didn't motivate anyone.

Decision

Walk people through three quick choices: what they want to work on, which food groups to focus on, and how they'd like to track it.

Outcome

People ended up with goals that felt like their own, and getting started was easier.

Prototype walkthrough

A three-step flow built around what motivates each user.

Decision · 03

Progress dashboard

Insight

It's hard to improve something you can't see.

Decision

Show progress for today, this week, and this month.

Outcome

People could glance at the dashboard and know what to do next.

3 progress views

Immediate feedback: see how today's intake tracks to goals.

06 · Reflection

Research is only as honest as its sample.

Our testers were younger and more tech-savvy than the people who actually use MyPlate. Before calling any of this finished, I'd test it again with a wider group.

What I learned

  • You have to design for motivation from the start.
  • Flexible inputs work better than strict ones.
  • A careful method can't make up for a narrow sample.

What I'd do next

  • Test again with a broader group of people.
  • Suggest goals based on how someone is doing.
  • Send reminders at the moments people usually decide what to eat.
